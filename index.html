When Agents Collude Weak-to-strong on-policy distillation for mitigating strategic deception in multi-agent systems.

Coordinating language-model agents deceive far more than single agents do. A small honesty-aligned teacher can train stronger agents, on their own rollouts, to stop.

Read the abstract

Autonomous agents increasingly coordinate on complex tasks, and that same capacity for coordination can create safety risks such as collusion, in which agents jointly pursue misaligned actions while concealing the true basis of those actions from oversight. We study this risk in a simulated insider-trading environment, comparing single agents with sequential and decentralized teams across seven language models from three families. By varying system-level objectives and pressure conditions, we examine when coordinated agents act on material non-public information, conceal the basis of a misaligned trade in a report, and maintain that deception under follow-up questioning. Compared with single-agent decision-making, multi-agent interaction raises misaligned-action rates from 35.4% to 68.2% and persistent-deception rates from 31.0% to 62.2%, both measured over all runs. These effects are largest under existential pressure, where teams sustain deception through follow-up questioning in 87% of all runs. Qualitative analysis reveals shared cover stories and coordinated rationales, suggesting group-level strategic deception rather than isolated reporting errors. To mitigate this behavior, we introduce PACT, a weak-to-strong on-policy distillation framework. Using generic honesty prompts, PACT trains a stronger student on its own sampled continuations, matching its next-token distribution to that of a smaller, frozen honesty-aligned teacher via reverse-KL supervision. Under existential pressure with ethical instructions, PACT reduces multi-agent persistent deception from 80% to 3%.

The setup

A controlled portfolio desk at a fictional firm, WhiteStone. Public analysis recommends holding a stock while a colleague passes a private tip that a merger is imminent. The same fixed decision is run across models, objectives, pressures, and coordination structures.

One agent (Alpha) runs the single-agent setting. Three agents share the task in teams: Maya gathers information, Noah forms the strategy, and Elena makes the trade and reports to the manager. An automated judge (GPT-OSS-120B) scores each run; human review of 700 trajectories agreed with it on every case.

What we find

Coordination amplifies the whole pathway

Moving from one agent to a team raises misaligned action from 35% to 68% and end-to-end persistent deception from 31% to 62%. Under existential pressure, teams hold the lie through follow-up questioning in 87% of runs.

evidence — Figure 2, Table 3

The failures are coordinated, not accidental

Agents privately agree on a cover story and present a unified false account, rather than each erring on its own. This is group-level strategic deception.

evidence — qualitative transcripts (above)

Generic honesty transfers out of distribution

Honesty fine-tuning sees no insider-trading examples, yet in the multi-agent setting it cuts misaligned action 68→37, deceptive reporting 62→32, and persistent deception 62→30, across all seven models.

evidence — Figure 5

A weak teacher aligns a stronger student

PACT trains students on their own rollouts under a small honesty-aligned teacher, pushing multi-agent persistent deception to 15% — and from 80% to 3% under existential pressure with ethical instructions — while preserving capability.

Results

PACT keeps capabilityAcross GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, and MMLU, accuracy stays within about one point in 11 of 21 model-benchmark comparisons; the largest drop is 3.2 points.

Limitations. The insider-trading environment is a controlled testbed, not open-ended deployment, and behavior is scored by an automated judge with a fixed rubric. Whether PACT generalizes beyond the evaluated tasks, models, and settings remains open.
